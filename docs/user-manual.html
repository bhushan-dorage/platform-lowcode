User Manual

Build applications.
No code required.

This guide is written for Business Analysts and other non-technical users who want to build, configure, and operate pages and workflows on the Platform Low-Code system — without writing a single line of code.

Business Analysts Operations Teams Product Owners

What can you build?

The platform is a toolkit for creating custom business applications — dashboards, data entry forms, approval workflows, and automated processes — entirely through configuration, not code.

AI Page Generation

Describe any dashboard or screen in plain English. The platform generates a fully structured page for you in seconds — ready to review, adjust, and publish.

Custom Dashboards

Compose pages from KPI cards, data tables, charts, forms, and text blocks — arranged in a multi-column grid without touching HTML or CSS.

Business Forms

Build multi-field forms with validation, conditional logic, and workflow triggers — deployable to end users immediately after publishing.

Approval Workflows

Route work through multi-step approval chains with automatic escalation, SLA timers, and email notifications — all configured without engineering support.

Business Data

Define and manage entities — customers, orders, contracts, or any business object — with a structured data model and full audit history.

Integrations

Connect to external systems — REST APIs, databases, email, Slack — using preconfigured connectors. No code, just configuration.

Who this guide is for

You do not need a technical background to use this manual. If you know how to use a spreadsheet or a CRM, you have all the skills you need. Technical decisions — infrastructure, security, integrations — are handled by your platform team.

Chapter 1

Getting Started

From your first login to navigating the main interface — everything you need to find your way around.

Logging in

The platform uses your organisation's identity system (Keycloak). You log in with the same credentials you use for other company applications — no separate password required.

1

Open the portal

Navigate to the portal URL provided by your platform team (for example, https://platform.yourcompany.com). You will be redirected to the login page automatically.

2

Enter your credentials

Use your company username and password. If your organisation uses single sign-on (SSO), click the appropriate button and authenticate with your existing account.

3

You're in

After logging in you arrive at the dashboard. Your name appears in the header. Your session stays active for 8 hours before requiring a re-login.

Navigating the portal

The portal has three main areas reachable from the top navigation bar:

AreaURL pathWhat you do here
Dashboard/dashboardOverview of platform activity and quick links to your most-used pages.
Task Inbox/tasksWork items assigned to you or your group — approvals, reviews, data-entry tasks.
Pages/pages/:keyPublished application screens. Each page has its own URL you can bookmark or share.

Your tenant and permissions

The platform supports multiple organisations (tenants) on shared infrastructure. Your account belongs to exactly one tenant, and you can only see your organisation's data. What you can do within that data depends on the roles your administrator has assigned to you — for example, whether you can publish pages or only draft them.

Need more access?

If you cannot see a feature described in this guide, your role may not include that permission. Contact your platform administrator or IT team to request the appropriate role.

Chapter 2

Task Inbox

The Task Inbox is where work finds you. Every approval, review, or data-entry request that's been routed to you — or your team — appears here.

Understanding tasks

A task is a unit of work created by a running workflow process. When a process reaches a human step — "review this request", "approve this budget", "enter missing data" — it creates a task and assigns it to the right person or group.

StatusWhat it means
UnassignedThe task belongs to your group but hasn't been picked up yet. Anyone in the group can claim it.
ClaimedYou (or a colleague) have taken ownership. Only the person who claimed it can complete it — unless they release it.
OverdueThe SLA deadline has passed. The task is highlighted and may have been escalated to a manager automatically.
CompletedThe work is done and the workflow has moved to its next step. Completed tasks are visible in history.

Claiming and completing a task

1

Open your inbox

Go to /tasks. You'll see a list of tasks assigned to you or your group, sorted by due date. Overdue items appear at the top with a warning indicator.

2

Click on a task to open it

The task detail view shows the task name, description, any attached data from earlier in the process (such as a submitted request form), and the due date.

3

Claim the task

Click Claim to take ownership. Once claimed, the task is removed from your colleagues' inboxes so two people don't work on the same item simultaneously.

4

Complete the task form

Most tasks include a form — fill in the required fields (approval decision, comments, additional data). Required fields are marked with an asterisk (*).

5

Submit

Click Complete Task. The workflow moves to its next step automatically — the requester is notified, and the next approver gets their own task if there's a further step.

Before you submit

Completing a task is final — you cannot edit a submitted response. If you realise you made an error after submitting, contact the requester or your administrator to restart the relevant workflow step.

Releasing a task you can't finish

If you've claimed a task but need someone else to handle it, use Release in the task detail view. The task returns to the group inbox and any colleague can pick it up.

Chapter 4

AI Page Generator

Describe the screen you need in plain English and the platform builds a fully structured page for you — KPI cards, data tables, charts, and forms — all arranged and configured automatically.

What the AI Page Generator does

Instead of designing a page layout yourself, you write a description of what the page should do — who will use it, what data it should show, and what actions it should support. The platform's AI reads your description and produces a complete page definition, which you can then preview, adjust, and save.

No code required

You don't need to know about widgets, schemas, or data sources. The AI takes care of the structure. Your job is to describe the business need clearly — the platform handles the rest.

Writing a good prompt

The quality of the generated page depends on how clearly you describe your need. A good prompt answers three questions:

1

Who is this page for?

Name the audience — operations manager, customer service agent, finance controller. This helps the AI choose the right level of detail and the right widgets.

2

What information should it show?

List the key metrics, tables, or data the user needs to see — for example: "total open orders, revenue this month, a table of orders by customer, a bar chart of orders by region."

3

What actions should it support?

If the user needs to do something — submit a form, enter data, filter a table — mention it. For example: "a form to create a new order" or "a searchable product table."

Example prompts

Weak promptStronger prompt
Order dashboard An order management dashboard for operations staff. Show KPIs for total open orders, revenue this week, and average fulfilment time. Include a table of recent orders with status badges, and a bar chart of orders by product category.
HR page An employee onboarding tracker for HR. Show the count of new joiners this month, a table of pending onboarding tasks with employee name and start date, and a text block with a link to the HR policy document.
Finance stuff A budget overview page for the Finance team. Show KPIs for total budget, spent to date, and remaining balance. Include a line chart of monthly spend against budget, and a table of the top 10 expense categories sorted by amount.

The AI generates sensible-looking data source URLs (such as /api/v1/entities/order) as placeholders. Widgets that point to a data source that doesn't exist on your system will display an empty state — they won't crash the page. Your platform team can update the data source URLs in the page definition to connect to your real data.

Chapter 5

Visual Page Builder

The Visual Page Builder is a drag-and-drop Studio tool that lets you compose pages manually — no AI, no API key, no internet connection required. Every widget and every configuration choice is under your direct control.

When to use the Visual Page Builder

Choose the Visual Page Builder when any of these apply:

  • Your team has a policy against sending business data to external AI services.
  • You don't have an AI Page Generator subscription or API key configured.
  • You need precise control over which widgets appear and how they are configured.
  • You want to edit a page that was previously generated by AI and fine-tune individual widgets.
  • You are working in an air-gapped or restricted-network environment.
Same output, different path

Pages built with the Visual Page Builder and pages created by the AI generator are identical once saved. Both use the same page schema, the same lifecycle (Draft → Published), and the same portal renderer. You can freely switch between the two tools.

Opening the Page Builder

The Visual Page Builder lives in the Studio application — the designer tool used to build platform artifacts. Open Studio and click Page Builder in the left navigation sidebar. To create a new page, the canvas starts empty. To edit an existing page, navigate directly to /pages/{pageKey} in Studio or open it from the artifact list.

The builder is divided into three areas:

  • Page Canvas (centre) — a live representation of your page, organised into sections.
  • Widget Properties (right) — configuration fields for the currently selected widget. Click any widget on the canvas to edit it here.

Step-by-step: building your first page

1

Name your page

At the top of the builder, type the page name in the large text field (e.g. Order Management Dashboard). The page key — the URL slug — is generated automatically from the name. You can edit the key manually while the page is still new; it locks permanently after the first save.

2

Add a section

Click + Add Section at the bottom of the canvas. A section is a horizontal row on the page. You can have as many sections as you need. Give each section an optional title and choose how many columns it should have (1, 2, 3, or 4) using the numbered buttons in the section header.

3

Drag a widget into the section

Click and hold a widget tile from the left palette, drag it over a section on the canvas, and release. The section highlights in blue when it's ready to accept the drop. The widget appears in the section and is automatically selected.

4

Configure the widget

The right panel shows the widget's properties. Every widget has a Title (optional display name) and a Column Span (how many columns of the section it occupies). Below those are type-specific settings — see the Configuring widgets section below for what to fill in for each type.

5

Reorder and remove widgets

Each widget card shows ← → arrows to move it left or right within the section, and an ✕ button to remove it. To delete an entire section, click its ✕ in the section header.

6

Save and publish

Click Save Draft to save without making the page visible to users, or click Publish to save and go live immediately. The page key locks after the first save — the URL will never change. A green ✓ message confirms the action succeeded.

Configuring each widget type

WidgetKey settingsWhat to enter
KPI Metric Label, Data Source URL, Value Field Label is the card's display name. URL is the API endpoint that returns the metric. Value Field is the JSON property containing the number (e.g. count). Ask your platform team for the correct URL and field name.
Table Data Source URL, Page Size, Columns URL returns a list of records. Page Size controls how many rows show per page (default 10). Add columns with + Add column: set the field name (JSON property) and a display header for each one.
Chart Chart Type, Data Source URL, Label Field, Value Field Choose Bar, Line, or Pie. URL returns the data series. Label Field is the category axis (e.g. month); Value Field is the number (e.g. revenue).
Form Form Key, Submit URL Form Key is the identifier of a form built in the Form Designer (e.g. create-order). Submit URL is the API endpoint that receives the form submission.
Text Variant, Content Choose a colour style — Default, Info (teal), Warning (amber), or Success (green). Type your message in the Content box. Markdown is not supported — keep it plain text.
Data source URLs

Unlike the AI generator — which provides placeholder URLs — the Visual Page Builder requires you to enter real API paths. If you leave a URL blank or enter an invalid path, the widget will render an empty state on the portal but won't crash the page. Coordinate with your platform team to confirm the correct endpoint paths before publishing.

Column spans explained

Each section has a column grid (1–4). A widget's Column Span controls how many of those columns it occupies. For example, in a 3-column section:

  • A KPI with span 1 takes up one-third of the row.
  • A Chart with span 2 takes up two-thirds.
  • A Table with span 3 fills the entire row.

The Column Span selector automatically limits itself to the section's column count — you can never set a span wider than the section allows.

Page key tip

The page key is derived from the page name automatically (spaces become hyphens, special characters are removed). If you want a custom key, edit it manually before saving — for example, change order-management-dashboard-q4 to orders. Once saved, the key is permanent.

Chapter 6

Page Widgets

Every page is built from widgets — reusable building blocks for different types of information. Understanding what each widget does helps you write better prompts and set correct expectations.

KPI

KPI Card

Displays a single number with a label — total orders, revenue, headcount. Supports a trend indicator (up/down arrow) and optional icon. Use for the most important metrics a user needs to see at a glance.

Table

Data Table

Displays rows and columns of data from a connected data source. Supports column types (text, date, status badge), configurable page size, and an optional search bar. Use for lists of records — orders, employees, tickets.

Chart

Chart

Visualises data as a bar chart, line chart, or pie chart. Reads from a data source with a label field and a value field. Use for trends over time, comparisons between categories, or share-of-total analysis.

Form

Embedded Form

Embeds a published form from the Form Service directly on the page. Ideal for data-entry pages where users need to submit information without leaving the dashboard context.

Text

Text / Announcement

A rich text block for instructions, announcements, policy notes, or contextual guidance. Comes in four visual styles: default (plain), info (teal), warning (amber), and success (green). Use to give users context they need to act correctly.

Widget layout: columns and spans

Pages are divided into sections, and each section has a column grid (1 to 4 columns wide). A widget can span multiple columns — a chart that needs more space might span 2 of 3 columns, leaving 1 column for a KPI card beside it.

When using the AI generator, you don't need to think about column spans — the AI chooses them based on your description. If you find a widget is too narrow or too wide after generation, ask your platform team to adjust the layout.

Chapter 7

Page Lifecycle

Every page moves through a defined lifecycle. Understanding the states helps you control when users see your changes.

Draft

Visible only to editors. The page exists but is not shown to end users. Safe to iterate here without affecting anyone's experience.

→

Published

Live for all users with access. The URL is active and the page can be bookmarked and shared.

→

Deprecated

Retired. The page is no longer shown to users and cannot be published again. Used to cleanly remove pages without deleting historical data.

Publishing a page

1

Open the page in draft

Navigate to the page URL (e.g. /pages/order-management-dashboard). In draft mode you see an editor banner at the top of the page.

2

Review the page

Check all widgets are showing the right content. Verify column layout looks correct on a typical screen size.

3

Click Publish

The page goes live immediately. A version number is recorded — every publish increments the version counter so you have a full history of what changed and when.

Version history

Every time you publish a page, a new version is recorded with a timestamp and the name of the person who published it. Your platform team can roll back to any previous version if something goes wrong after a change.

Deprecating a page

When a page is no longer needed, deprecate it rather than leaving it live. This removes it from users' navigation and bookmarks but preserves the historical record. You cannot undo a deprecation — if in doubt, leave the page in Draft and discuss with your platform team first.

Chapter 8

Forms

Forms are the primary way users enter data on the platform — submitting a request, recording an outcome, updating a record, or completing a task.

What a form is

A form is a structured data-entry screen built by your platform team using the Form Service. Each form has a unique form key (like purchase-request) and one or more fields. Forms can be embedded inside pages using the Form widget, or they can appear as the completion screen for a task in your inbox.

Filling in a form

Forms on the platform behave like any web form — click a field, type your input, and use the tab key to move between fields. A few things to know:

Field indicatorMeaning
* (asterisk)Required — you must fill this in before submitting.
Hint textGrey text below a field giving guidance on the expected format or acceptable values.
Validation errorA red message beneath a field explaining why your entry isn't accepted (e.g. "Must be a valid email address").
Conditional fieldSome fields appear only when a previous answer triggers them — for example, a "Reason for exception" field that shows only when "No" is selected on an approval question.
Before submitting

Form submissions are permanent. Once you click Submit, your response is recorded in the audit trail and triggers the next step in the workflow. Double-check your entries, especially numeric values and dates, before submitting.

Viewing submissions

After submitting a form you receive an on-screen confirmation. Depending on how your platform is configured, you may also receive an email receipt. To view a list of your past submissions, ask your platform administrator to add a table widget pointing to your form submission data to a relevant page.

Chapter 9

Workflows & Processes

Workflows automate multi-step business processes — from a simple two-step approval to a complex multi-department process with branching logic, parallel paths, and SLA timers.

What a process looks like

A process is a sequence of steps that the platform executes automatically, pausing only when it needs human input. Think of it as a flowchart that the system follows:

1

Start event

Something triggers the process — a form submission, an API call, a schedule, or a manual start by a user.

2

Automated steps

The system may automatically fetch data, send notifications, call external systems, or update records — all without human involvement.

3

Human tasks

When the process needs a decision or data entry from a person, it creates a task and parks itself until the task is completed. This is what you see in your Task Inbox.

4

End event

The process concludes — records are updated, notifications are sent to relevant parties, and the process instance is archived.

Starting a process

Processes are typically started in one of two ways:

  • Submitting a form — the form submission itself triggers the process. The confirmation screen will tell you that a process has been started.
  • Clicking a Start Process button — on a page where a process has been made directly startable, a button appears that launches the process immediately (sometimes with a brief setup form).

SLAs and deadlines

Many tasks carry an SLA — a time limit within which the task must be completed. When a deadline is approaching, the task appears in your inbox with an amber warning. When the deadline has passed, it turns red. Some processes are configured to automatically escalate overdue tasks to a manager.

Monitor process status

If you started a process and want to check where it is — which step it's on, whether it's waiting for someone else to act — ask your platform team to add a process status panel to a relevant page. The platform tracks the full status of every running process instance.

Chapter 10

Data & Entities

The platform has a built-in data layer where your organisation's business objects — customers, orders, contracts, products — are stored and managed as entities.

What is an entity?

An entity is a business object with a fixed structure — for example, an Order entity might have fields for customer name, order date, total value, and status. Your platform team defines the structure; you and your colleagues create, view, and update records through pages and forms.

Working with entity data

As a business user, you interact with entity data through widgets on pages:

What you want to doHow to do it
View a list of recordsUse a Table widget on a page connected to the entity data source.
See summary statisticsUse a KPI widget pointing to a count or aggregate of entity records.
Create a new recordUse a Form widget configured to submit to the entity's create endpoint.
Update an existing recordUse a task form inside a workflow, or a form pre-populated with an existing record's data.

Audit trail

Every create, update, and delete action on entity data is recorded in the platform's audit log. The audit trail captures who made the change, when, from which IP address, and what the previous value was. This record is tamper-evident — it cannot be altered after the fact — making it suitable for regulatory compliance requirements.

Data residency

Your organisation's data never leaves your tenancy — entities, audit records, and form submissions all belong exclusively to your tenant and are invisible to other organisations on the shared platform.

Reference

Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms you'll encounter on the platform.

  • Audit trail A permanent, tamper-evident record of every action taken on the platform — who did what, and when. Used for compliance and security investigations.
  • Claim (a task) Taking personal ownership of a task from your group inbox so that only you can complete it. Prevents two people from working on the same item at once.
  • Connector A configured link between the platform and an external system — for example, a REST API, a database, an email server, or a Slack channel. Set up by your platform team.
  • Draft A page status meaning the page has been created but is not visible to end users yet. You can iterate freely on a draft without affecting anyone's experience.
  • Entity A structured business object stored on the platform — such as an Order, Customer, or Contract. Entities have defined fields and are managed through pages and forms.
  • Form A data-entry screen with defined fields and validation rules. Forms are built by your platform team and embedded in pages or presented as task completion screens.
  • Page A configurable application screen made up of widgets arranged in sections. Pages are stored as configuration — publishing a new page requires no code deployment.
  • Page key The unique identifier for a page, used in its URL — for example, the page key order-dashboard produces the URL /pages/order-dashboard. Chosen when the page is created.
  • Process An automated multi-step workflow that the platform executes, pausing at human task steps. Also called a workflow instance.
  • Publish Moving a page from Draft to Published — making it live and accessible to all users with the appropriate permission. A published page is versioned.
  • Section A horizontal grouping of widgets on a page, with its own column grid (1–4 columns). Pages are composed of one or more sections stacked vertically.
  • SLA Service Level Agreement — the time limit within which a task must be completed. Tasks approaching or past their SLA deadline are flagged with a warning in the inbox.
  • Tenant Your organisation's isolated environment on the platform. Data in your tenant is completely separate from any other organisation using the same system.
  • Visual Page Builder A drag-and-drop Studio tool for building pages without AI. You compose pages by dragging widget tiles onto sections and configuring them in a properties panel. Produces the same page schema as the AI Page Generator.
  • Widget A reusable building block on a page — KPI card, data table, chart, form, or text block. Widgets are configured (not coded) and can be combined freely to build any screen.
  • Workflow A pre-defined sequence of automated and human steps that handles a business process — approvals, onboarding, exception handling — consistently and with a full audit record.
